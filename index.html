01 / Train with intent

Your next session,
sorted.

Simple gym templates for generally healthy adults. Start light, learn the movements and build a routine you can repeat.

Start here / 2 days a week

Full-body foundation

Repeat this session on two nonconsecutive days, such as Monday and Thursday. A qualified trainer can help you learn each movement.

Leg press
1 × 12–15
Seated leg curl
1 × 12–15
Machine chest press
1 × 12–15
Seated cable row
1 × 12–15
Machine shoulder press
1 × 12–15
Calf raise
1 × 12–15
Dead bug
1 × 6 / side

Warm up with 5–10 minutes of easy walking first. Rest about a minute between exercises, or longer if needed. Use a comfortable load and stop before form breaks down.

Back-day option / Gym

Pull with control

A back-focused session to fit into an existing balanced routine. It does not replace training your legs, chest, shoulders and core.

Lat pulldown
1 × 12–15
Chest-supported row
1 × 12–15
Seated cable row
1 × 12–15

Start with one controlled set of each. Rest about a minute between exercises. Avoid training the same muscles on consecutive days.

Progress, not ego.

Write down your weight and reps. When the set feels easier with clean technique, increase the load gradually. If you lose control, lower it again.

General examples, not a personalised prescription. Stop if an exercise causes pain. If you have an injury, medical condition or are new to lifting, get individual guidance from a qualified professional.

02 / Fuel the work

Diet plans.
Real food first.

No crash diets. No magic foods. These example days use familiar meals, with vegetarian swaps. Portions depend on your body, activity and needs.

Build / Muscle-gain basics

Eat to support training

Pair strength training with regular, balanced meals. If weight gain is appropriate for you, add food gradually rather than forcing huge meals.

Example day
Breakfast
Oats with milk or fortified soy milk, a banana and eggs or tofu.
Lunch
Rice, fish or chickpea curry, vegetables and plain curd or a soy alternative.
Snack
Fruit with plain yoghurt or a soy alternative, plus a small handful of nuts.
Dinner
Chapati, chicken or dal, and vegetables.

Need more food? Add a snack or an extra serving of rice, oats, nuts or dairy as suited to your needs. Weight gain is not all muscle, and results vary.

Lean / Fat-loss basics

Keep the food. Adjust the balance.

If weight loss is appropriate for you, aim for a sustainable reduction in calories. Keep protein and vegetables in your meals instead of skipping whole food groups.

Example day
Breakfast
Idli with sambar, plus eggs or tofu for protein.
Lunch
Roughly half a plate of vegetables, with fish or beans and a smaller serving of rice.
Snack
Whole fruit and plain curd, or roasted chickpeas.
Dinner
Vegetables, chicken or dal, and a portion of chapati or rice.

Measure cooking oil, reduce sugary drinks and adjust portions to your needs. There is no need to ban rice or chapati.

Make it fit your life.

Include a protein source at meals, vary your fruit and vegetables, and drink water regularly. Swap foods for allergies, preferences and budget. These examples are not calorie- or macro-calculated plans.

For adults only. Not medical nutrition advice. If you are pregnant, under 18, have a health condition, a prescribed diet or a history of an eating disorder, speak with a doctor or registered dietitian before changing your diet or weight.

03 / Better reps

Small cues.
Better sessions.

01

Pulldown, not swing

Keep your back straight and pull smoothly toward the front of your chest. Never pull behind your neck. Return the bar slowly; avoid using momentum.

02

Earn the next plate

Use a load you can control through the movement. If your technique changes to finish the rep, reduce the weight. Ask a qualified trainer to check your form.

03

Recover to repeat

Do not train the same muscles on consecutive days. Keep a simple training log so you can build on your last session, rather than guessing.
